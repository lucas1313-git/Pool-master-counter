🎱 8 Ball Punishment — The Rules of Engagement

Goal of the game

Punishment is designed to stimulate players to perform exceptional shots in the course of a normal 8-Ball game. The game requires players to consistently improve their skill set in rails, kicks, and smart leaves.

The break

The break is as a regular 8-Ball game. If a ball is pocketed by the breaking player, a second ball should "cover" the initial ball. The player has the choice of ball category, high or low.

If a fault is performed while breaking, the breaker loses the break privilege and receives a 1-ball punishment. If a ball is pocketed during the faulty break, that ball stays put. The following player will have ball in hand and may choose the category to play.

The game play

The game proceeds as a regular 8-Ball game, as stipulated in the rules of the American Billiards Association, but for the following differences.

Punishments

In order to stimulate the players to perform difficult shots, these will be rewarded by punishing the opponent as follows. By punishment we mean that the opponent will remove one of their balls from the table, and place it in line from the break mark. If the player has no ball to remove, a "debt" will be constructed, in direct relation with the ball the player is being punished for.

  • 1 — Combos: A normal combo (2 balls) is rewarded 1 punishment ball to the opponent. Similarly, a 3-ball combo is rewarded 2 balls of punishment, and so on for a 4-5-6 ball combo, rewarding an increasing quantity — respectively 3-4-5 (the first ball is not counted in the equation).
  • 2 — Rails: A rail is considered "punishable" if it is not in line with the targeted pocket on the last bounce. These non-punishable rails are called "small rails" and are also considered null for the call — for example, if a player calls a rail and the ball touches the same rail near the pocket before getting in, that last touch is not counted either in the call or in the punishment count.
    A single rail is rewarded by a 1-ball punishment to the opponent; a double rail is rewarded by a 2-ball punishment, and so on. If a player calls 2 rails and the ball hits 3 rails (not counting a small rail), the shot is considered a fault, and the player should remove the faulty ball and an extra ball as a punishment for the fault.
  • 3 — Faults: Any fault, including a sloppy ball (a ball getting in the pocket without being called), is subject to punishment. In the case of a sloppy ball, the ball is removed from the pocket and the player receives an extra punishment of one ball. The opponent always gets ball in hand for a fault.
    Scratching is a fault. If a player scratches while successfully getting a ball in, the ball goes back out and the player receives a punishment. If the player was attempting a rail or a combo and scratches, the reward is voided; the opponent gets ball in hand.
    Scratching on the 8-ball is a normal fault, except if the cue ball also gets pocketed — in that case the game is forfeited and the opponent wins. The winner counts all balls left on the table and adds them as one unit to the total score; no extra winning point is awarded in that circumstance (the extra winning point is a reward reserved for a player who legally pockets the 8-ball to end the game — see "The 8-ball," below).

Calling shots for extra punishment

Calling 4 balls: A player may "call 4" upon pocketing 4 balls in a row on the same turn — the call must be made before the fourth shot to be rewarded. A successful call earns a punishment to the opponent. Missing a "call 4" is not itself a fault.

Calling 5-6-7 or 8 balls: The same idea applies to a player pushing their run further. However, missing a "call 5" nullifies the reward already earned from the "call 4" — and missing a "call 6" or "call 7" nullifies every reward from the calls before it. Every successful call from 4 through 7 earns an extra punishment ball to the opponent; missing such a call is not a fault beyond losing the rewards already banked.

Calling 2 going in together: On a combo, a player may "call 2 in the pocket" — if the combo succeeds and the precedent ball also drops (not just the targeted ball), a punishment is earned. Missing this call is not a fault, but the player does not shoot again even if the combo itself succeeds, and the opponent is not punished either way. The same call can also be made on a kiss (getting the first ball in, kissing out, and pocketing another ball while calling the sequence correctly) for the same punishment reward; missing it forfeits the turn even if the original ball drops, and the ball is nullified even toward a "call 4-5-6-7-8" run in progress.

The 8-ball

To win the game, a player must rail the 8-ball or pocket it after a minimum of 2 kicks — rails or kicks off the adjacent (small) rail don't count. The winner is rewarded an extra point for the win, plus all the balls left on the table, including every accumulated punishment ball, counted together as the win.

Getting the punishment balls back on the table

Punishment and faulty balls are pulled out after the player finishes their turn, lined up in this order:

  1. All "debt accumulated" balls — the balls a player would owe if they went under 0, including the opponent's debt balls where applicable.
  2. The awarded punishment balls of the opponent.
  3. The faulty ball(s) — starting with the 8-ball first, in the case of a scratch on it.

Any mechanical fault, including disturbing the game while handling the bridge, is a punishment — the player loses their turn, gets punished, and the opponent gets ball in hand. The opponent shall not pull balls out of the pocket while a player is taking their turn; interfering with play by removing balls from the pockets is itself a fault and is punishable.
